01 — What it does

Two questions every traveler already asks

“Where was this?”

Real places, not coordinates

Import photos and they land on the actual pub, park, trailhead, and temple — not a pair of coordinates in a field. You confirm each pin, because GPS drifts, and a pin in the wrong pub is worse than no pin. In the example atlas, 25 photos resolved to 21 named places.

“How much of the world have I actually seen?”

The globe + coverage

Your list of countries is free, as plain text. Max turns it into a globe that colors in as you travel, plus coverage stats — how much of a city you have actually walked, not just visited. The example atlas colors 3 countries on 3 continents. The rest stays parchment until you've been.

“Who else can see this?”

Postcards — friends only

Postcards is a feed with only your friends in it. No followers. No discover page. Nothing public by default. A trip leaves your phone only when you send it to someone yourself — a whole trip, photos, places, and routes, landing in their atlas.

02 — Private by design

Said plainly, including the part most apps skip

It lives on your iPhone

Your atlas is stored on your phone and works fully offline — plane mode, no signal, halfway up a mountain. An account is optional; it exists only for sharing with friends, if you ever want it. Your trips never depend on a server.

Your data is never paywalled

Full backup and export are free on every tier, forever. Your photos, places, and routes are yours — leave whenever, take everything with you. No hostage pricing, no "restore is a premium feature."

The feed has a door, and it's closed

Postcards shows only people you've shared with. There is no public feed, no follower count, and no setting that accidentally makes a trip public — public isn't a thing Waypoint does.

The honest clause, about ads

The free tier is supported by non-personalized ads — ads that are never targeted using your trips, your location, or a profile of you, because that data never reaches them. Max and the Max trial carry no ads at all. This page, for the record, has no trackers, no analytics, and no cookies.

04 — Questions

Asked, answered

When does Waypoint launch?

Soon — we're finishing the Postcards feed and the 3D globe, and we launch when they're ready, not before. No date is promised, on purpose. Waitlist members hear first, and the first 500 get TestFlight invites while we polish.

How much does it cost?

Free — your map, your trips, every import path. Max ($9.99/month or $79.99/year) adds the globe, coverage stats, unlimited photos, cloud sync, and removes the ads. At launch, Max starts free: one month on monthly billing, three months on annual. Either way, exporting your own data is always free. That's a promise, not a tier.

Is the feed public? Can strangers see my trips?

No. There is no public feed, no followers, and no discover page. Your Postcards feed shows only people you've shared with, and a trip leaves your phone only when you send it to someone yourself.

Do I need an account?

No. The atlas works with no account at all — it's on your phone. An account exists only for sharing: friends, the Postcards feed, and (on Max) cloud sync. Skip it and nothing about your own trips changes.

I lost my Google Timeline. Can Waypoint get it back?

If you have a Google Takeout export, yes — import it and Waypoint rebuilds your trips from it. If you never exported, your camera roll still holds most of the story: import your photos and the trips come back. Our confirmation email links a 2-minute guide to grabbing your Takeout file while you still can.

Is there an Android version?

Not at launch — iPhone first, done properly. Android is on the roadmap, and waitlist members will be the first to know. (Yes, you can still join the list just for the launch news.)
