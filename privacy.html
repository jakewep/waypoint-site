Waypoint Privacy Policy

Updated 29 October 2026

The short version

Your atlas lives on your iPhone and works fully offline. An account is optional, and it exists only for sharing: friends, the Postcards feed, and — on Max — cloud sync. Data leaves your phone only through doors you open: posting or sharing, turning sync on, or buying Max. We don’t sell data, we don’t track you across apps, and Waypoint contains no analytics SDK. The free version shows non-personalized ads (see Advertising); Max is completely ad-free, always.

Your data is yours

You can export everything — every trip, place, walk and photo — at any time, free, on every plan, in open formats (a restorable Waypoint backup, GPX for walks). We will never paywall your existing data, and we will never move a feature that was free behind the paywall. Paid plans sell new capability, never access to what you already made.

What stays on your device

Trips, trip dates and names, copies of photos you add, GPS tracks of walks you record, the places you confirm, your itinerary plans and your settings. These are kept in Waypoint’s private storage and included in your normal iPhone/iCloud device backup. Deleting the app, or Settings › Delete all data, removes them from the phone. Nothing in this list is sent anywhere unless you share it, post it, or turn on sync (see below).

Accounts

Waypoint works with no account at all. So it can tell your device apart from anyone else’s, the app creates an anonymous guest ID on first launch; it carries no name and nothing about you. If you sign in with Apple, that identity is linked to your guest ID — same atlas, same data, now with a name you choose: a unique @username and a display name. Your bio, home location and favorite country are optional extras you can add and hide field by field; your home location is text you type, never read from GPS. An account unlocks friends, the feed and sync. It is never required to use the atlas.

Sharing and the Postcards feed

Nothing is shared unless you share it. Trips you send as files (Messages, AirDrop, Mail…) are still created on your device and never touch our servers. Sharing inside the app is different, and here is exactly what it sends: offering a trip to a friend uploads that trip’s details — name, dates, places, walk routes — so they can accept it; accepting makes a copy in their atlas. Posting a postcard uploads the caption and photos you pick, as reduced copies re-encoded with the location data inside the photo file stripped out; the place names you confirmed ride along, because they are the post. Postcards are visible to your friends only, unless you mark one public. Comments, likes and the reports and blocks you file are stored with your account so the feed can work and stay safe.

Cloud sync (Max)

Sync is a Max feature and it is off until you turn it on. With sync on, Waypoint keeps a copy of your atlas on our servers — trips, places, activities and walk tracks, and your original photo files in private storage that only your account can open — so a new phone can restore your atlas and your devices stay in step. Sync is a copy, not a move: your phone stays the working atlas, and everything keeps working offline. Turning sync off, or Max lapsing, stops new uploads; the existing cloud copy is kept, untouched, until you delete your account (see Deletion).

Location

Waypoint uses your location while you record a walk or an activity, while trip tracking you started is running (including in the background, if you allow “Always”), and to name places. It is not collected at any other time. Where a walk went exists only inside that walk: it reaches our servers only if you share or post the trip it belongs to, or sync is on. The places you confirm are stored with their coordinates — that is what a place is — and follow the same rule.

Photos and camera

You choose which photos to add. Waypoint reads their date and location to build trips, and after a recorded walk it looks for photos taken during that walk. Copies are stored on your device. A photo leaves the device only inside something you share or post (as a stripped, reduced copy — see Sharing) or, with sync on, as an original file in your private sync storage. Camera access is used only when you take a photo during a walk.

Motion & notifications

If you turn on walk prompts, Waypoint uses Motion & Fitness activity while the app is open to notice when you are walking, and shows a local notification. This data never leaves your device. Trip reminders and trial reminders are also local. If you allow remote notifications, Waypoint stores your device’s push token on our servers so it can deliver friend requests, comments on your postcards and trip offers — nothing else — and you can switch each kind off in Settings.

Advertising

The free version of Waypoint is supported by ads served by Google AdMob. These ads are non-personalized: we collect no personal information for ads, ads are not targeted using your identity, your location, or your trips, and there is no cross-app tracking — Waypoint never shows Apple’s tracking prompt because it never asks to track you. Google may process device information needed to deliver ads and prevent fraud, under Google’s privacy policy. Your trips, photos, walks and location history are never passed to the ad service. The paid plan, Max, is completely ad-free.

Services Waypoint uses

Place names and maps come from Apple Maps; Apple receives the coordinates needed to answer those requests under Apple’s privacy policy. The shaded “how much of this city have you covered” outline is fetched from OpenStreetMap’s Nominatim service using the city name only — no account, no identity, nothing about your trips. Sign-in and purchases run through Apple. Purchases are managed through RevenueCat, which receives your purchase history and, once you sign in, your Waypoint account ID so Max unlocks on the account — no trips, photos or location. Our own servers are hosted by Supabase and hold only what the sections above describe: accounts, shared and posted content, and sync copies. On the free version, ads are served by Google AdMob as described in Advertising above.

Referrals

When someone new to Waypoint adds a trip you sent, Waypoint’s referral service receives both people’s anonymous purchase IDs (no names, trips, photos or locations) to check the referral and grant each of you a month of Max. Records are kept only to prevent abuse.

Deletion

Two different deletes, both in the app. “Delete all data” (Settings › Your data) wipes the atlas on this iPhone. “Delete account” (You › Account) permanently removes everything our servers hold for you: your profile and @username (the name is freed for anyone to claim), synced trips, photos and walks including uploaded photo files, your postcards, comments and likes, friendships and trip shares, and your plan record; we keep only a minimal note that a deletion happened. Places you confirmed stay listed as public places, with your name removed. Deleting your account does not delete the atlas on this iPhone — it’s yours, and it stays — and it does not cancel a Max subscription: Apple handles billing, so manage or cancel it under Settings › Apple ID › Subscriptions.

Children

Waypoint is not directed at children under 13.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will appear in the app and at the published URL with a new date. Questions: use Contact support in Waypoint’s settings.
